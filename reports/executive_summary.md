# Executive Summary — Customer Value & Retention

**Scope:** UK online gift retailer · Dec 2009 – 9 Dec 2011 · £18.93M net revenue · 5,875 identified customers

## What we found
1. **A third of customers carry the business.** The most valuable, still-active third generates **81%** of revenue and is predicted to generate **78%** of next year's customer value (£8.14M total).
2. **New customers rarely come back.** Only **23%** of first-time buyers order again the following month. Customers acquired in the Christmas peak are the least loyal (as low as **9%**).
3. **Last year's growth was not new demand.** November 2011 revenue was +2.3% vs 2010 because fewer orders were cancelled, not because more were placed.
4. **Customers shop by collection.** Buyers of one Retrospot / Polkadot / Vintage Christmas item are 17–25× more likely than average to buy another item from the same theme.

## What to do
| Priority | Action | Expected effect | How we'll know |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Protect the top third without discounts** — loyalty perks, early access | Avoid giving away ~8% of retail revenue on purchases that would happen anyway | Retention rate of this group |
| 2 | **Turn first purchases into second purchases** — "complete your collection" emails to active, low-spend customers | This group holds 14% of next year's predicted value vs 6% of past revenue | 2nd-purchase rate vs 10% holdout |
| 3 | **Pilot win-back before scaling** — time-limited offer to high-value lapsing customers, best first by CLV | Profitable only if ≥ ~4% of contacted retail customers return because of the offer | Reactivation rate vs holdout |
| 4 | **Automate the long tail** — email only for low-value lapsed customers | Near-zero cost | Open/click, reactivation |
| 5 | **Merchandise by theme** — "Shop the collection" on product pages | Larger baskets | Average order value |

## Caveats
Customer value forecasts are for **ranking customers**, not for the revenue budget (they run ~2% high and ignore seasonality). Campaign economics use assumed margin (30%) and cost (£5/customer) and must be validated with a holdout test.
